We need to compute the proper time experienced by the astronaut traveling at v = 0.99999987c.  

The distance from the Large Magellanic Cloud to Earth is about 160,000 light-years. However, the problem doesn't explicitly give the distance—it gives the alien lifetime and the astronaut's age. The key is that the astronaut's clock runs slow due to time dilation.  

The Lorentz factor γ = 1 / sqrt(1 - v²/c²).  
v/c = 0.99999987 → (v/c)² ≈ 0.99999974.  
1 - (v/c)² ≈ 2.6 × 10⁻⁷.  
γ ≈ 1 / sqrt(2.6 × 10⁻⁷) ≈ 1 / (5.099 × 10⁻⁴) ≈ 1961.  

If the distance to Earth is about 160,000 light-years, then in Earth's frame the travel time is about 160,000 years. But the astronaut's proper time is that divided by γ:  
160,000 / 1961 ≈ 81.6 years.  

The astronaut is 22 years old, so after 81.6 years they would be about 103.6 years old, which is less than the 150-year average lifetime, so they survive.  

Thus the answer is approximately 81 years.
